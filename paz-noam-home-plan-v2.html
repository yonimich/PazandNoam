The brief

Overview

Paz and Noam live in a rented three-bedroom flat in North West London with their son Bar, who is three. Their second baby is due in January 2027, and the home needs to be ready before then.

Challenges & problems

  • Two children, one bedroom: Bar and the baby need to share, with space to sleep, play and change nappies
  • Toys are spreading into the living room, and it is starting to feel like a playroom
  • The flat is rented: limited drilling, built-ins stay, and the landlord's double bed can't be taken apart or removed
  • Radiators sit under the windows, which limits where furniture, covers and the cot can go
  • A tight deadline: everything has to be finished before the baby arrives, with no work once the baby is home
  • Storage is mostly open shelving today, so things are on show

Special requests

  • Both kids in the same room
  • Everything hidden away in closed storage
  • The living room must never become toyland; a closed toy cabinet there is fine
  • A proper kids' bed for Bar, around 160 × 90
  • Keep the things you love: the play kitchen, the chest of drawers, the floor lamp and the shelf

Requirements

  • Budget: as cheap as possible, but willing to invest in the right piece
  • Ready before January 2027; only the cot is added after the birth
  • Rental-friendly: freestanding furniture, removable options, landlord OK for any fixings
  • Child safety: tall furniture anchored, window restrictors, cord-free window dressings, safe cot placement
  • Enough storage for a 3-year-old's toys plus a growing baby's clothes and kit

Main plan vs alternative

Recommended

Main plan: swap the rooms

The kids move into the larger study; the landlord's bed moves to Bar's room, which becomes the study and guest room.

  • Room for both beds, the changing chest and a play area
  • Toy storage and the play kitchen stay in the kids' room
  • Space for a rocking chair (Layout B)
  • The guest room stays calm, with no toys

Costs: moving the landlord's bed and more set-up work

Less recommended

Alternative: keep Bar's room

Bar's room becomes the shared kids' room; the study stays the study and guest room.

  • No space for a rocking chair
  • Less storage and no play area
  • Toy storage and the play kitchen move to the study
  • The study becomes the playroom, with no space for it

In its favour: Bar keeps his room, the landlord's bed doesn't move, and it's less work

Layout B

Use the Layout B button: the hall storage goes, the play kitchen moves into the hall, and low cube storage wraps the corner by Bar's bed and under the window up to the radiator. A rocking chair (or bean bag) sits in the corner against the corridor wall, next to the cot, for feeds and stories.

Look

Beige slat panelling at 120 cm with a ledge, a slatted radiator cover, linen curtains on an oak pole (blackout lined, no cords), a warm LED strip under the panelling ledge, art rails for Bar's drawings, woodland prints above the cot and vehicle prints above Bar's bed.

Safety

Anchor the tall chest, fit window restrictors, add a clip-on bed guard, and use lightweight acrylic frames above the cot.

Kids' room

In the alternative, Bar stays in his own room and the baby joins him. Nothing moves between rooms, but the room is smaller, so the cot, the changing chest and Bar's bed take nearly all the floor.

What we lose by keeping Bar's room as the kids' room

  • No space for a rocking chair or feeding seat
  • Less storage: no cube unit, and the only drawers are the baby's changing chest
  • No play area: the floor between the beds is just a walkway
  • Toy storage moves to a different room (the study)
  • The play kitchen moves to a different room (the study hall)

Effectively the study becomes the playroom, but with the double bed, the desk and the chest in it, there is no space for one.

Sleeping

Bar's new 80 × 160 bed sits on the right wall between the two built-ins, clear of the big wardrobe's doors. The cot goes on the left wall, about 1 m across from Bar's bed.

Changing

A white chest of drawers with a changing mat on top stands under the side window at the foot of the cot. It covers the bottom of that window, so fit a window restrictor.

Clothes

The big built-in holds Bar's clothes; the small built-in takes the baby's. The corner by the built-ins stays empty so the doors open freely.

Look

The same beige slat panelling on the plain walls, a slatted radiator cover in the bay and a small round rug between the beds. Bamboo blinds on the bay windows.

Safety

Anchor the changing chest, fit window restrictors (the chest sits under a window) and add a bed guard if the bed has no rail.
